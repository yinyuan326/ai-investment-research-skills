# Source and citation standard

## 1. Source plan

Before browsing, list the decision-critical questions and preferred primary source for each. For a public company, start with exchange or regulator filings, annual and interim reports, prospectuses, earnings-call materials, official customer or procurement records, government statistics, technical standards, patents, and environmental or safety records.

Use aggregators to locate originals, not as the final citation when an official source is reasonably available.

## 2. Citation format

Place the citation next to the claim. For a filing or long PDF, include document title, publication date, page number, and link:

`[公司2025年年度报告，2026-04-25，p.87](https://example.com/filing.pdf)`

For a volatile webpage or market price, include the observation time and access date. For a dataset, distinguish publication date from the period described.

Do not cite a search-results page or a source that merely repeats the claim without evidence.

## 3. Decision-critical claims

The evidence matrix must include every claim that can materially alter:

- market size or demand timing;
- customer qualification, backlog, framework contracts, or revenue visibility;
- technology, capacity, yield, cost, or competitive advantage;
- historical normalization or forecast assumptions;
- valuation inputs, catalysts, risks, or conclusion.

For a formal report, a matrix with only a handful of headline claims is insufficient. Coverage, not an arbitrary bibliography length, is the test.

## 4. Management claims

Label market share, performance, customer acceptance, qualification, capacity, yield, cost advantage, and commercialization timing as `[公司口径]` unless independently verified. Seek corroboration from customer or supplier records, procurement, technical documentation, regulator data, named industry datasets, or observable financial outcomes.

If independent corroboration is unavailable:

1. retain the company attribution;
2. lower confidence;
3. state the missing verification;
4. avoid using the claim as the sole basis for a forecast or premium valuation.

## 5. Contradictions and source incentives

Record credible contradictory evidence and explain which interpretation is more persuasive. Disclose when an industry source sells services, when an expert has an incentive, or when company guidance changed. Never silently average estimates with incompatible definitions.

## 6. Reproducibility

For each `[计算]`, show source inputs, units, formula, and period. Preserve enough detail for another analyst to reproduce market size, forecast, enterprise-to-equity bridge, diluted shares, per-share value, and return calculations.

# Research method

## 1. Governing principles

Make the work decision-useful, traceable, comparable, current enough for the decision, and explicit about uncertainty. Apply securities-research, financial-analysis, and commercial-diligence discipline without claiming regulated-research compliance.

- Evidence precedes narrative.
- Forecasts derive from operating drivers, not desired valuation outcomes.
- Industry growth matters only through a demonstrated path to company revenue, margins, cash flow, and returns.
- Supporting and adverse evidence receive equal scrutiny.
- Actual, adjusted, forecast, and scenario figures remain distinct.
- A polished report cannot compensate for a non-reproducible model.

## 2. Evidence hierarchy and triangulation

Prefer sources in this order while considering relevance and recency:

1. audited filings, prospectuses, exchange and regulator records, court records, government statistics, procurement and contract records;
2. official technical documents, patents, standards, peer-reviewed papers, safety and environmental records;
3. company investor materials, earnings calls, announcements, and executive statements;
4. named and methodologically transparent industry datasets;
5. reputable reporting with named sources or independently corroborated documents;
6. expert, customer, supplier, channel, employee, and trade commentary;
7. aggregators, anonymous posts, promotional material, and search snippets.

Treat company statements as primary evidence of what management says, not independent proof. For a decision-critical market-share, customer, technology, qualification, cost, or capacity claim, seek at least one independent corroborating source. If none exists, label the claim `[公司口径]`, lower confidence, and state what would verify it.

For each major thesis pillar, seek three different evidence types where reasonably available: company or filing evidence, external industry or technical evidence, and observable financial or operating evidence.

## 3. Claim taxonomy

- `[事实]`: directly supported by verifiable evidence. Cite it.
- `[公司口径]`: management target, performance claim, market-share claim, customer statement, or forecast. Attribute and cite it.
- `[计算]`: arithmetic derived from disclosed inputs. Show formula, units, periods, and source inputs.
- `[判断]`: interpretation, forecast, scenario, causal inference, or investment view. State supporting and contrary evidence.

Split mixed sentences. Do not let a verified fact silently convert an adjacent judgment into a fact.

## 4. Claim ledger

Maintain a working ledger before drafting:

| ID | Material claim | Label | Source/date/page | Data period | Confidence | Corroboration | Contradiction/limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | Example | 事实 | Filing, 2026-03-01, p.87 | FY2025 | High | Regulator data | JV excluded |

Include every claim that can materially change market size, competitive position, operating forecasts, financial quality, valuation, risk, or conclusion. A bibliography without claim-to-source mapping is insufficient.

## 5. Industry-to-company bridge

Build the bridge from the customer problem and physical or economic demand unit:

1. define market boundaries, geography, product specification, and time period;
2. estimate end-market units or customer spend using top-down and bottom-up methods;
3. estimate content, usage, replacement cycle, or spend per unit;
4. apply qualification, adoption, capacity, regulation, and route-to-market constraints;
5. estimate serviceable and realistically obtainable share;
6. translate share into company volume, price, revenue, gross profit, capex, and working capital;
7. reconcile the result with disclosed capacity, utilization, backlog, customers, and historical revenue.

Show ranges when inputs are uncertain. Do not use TAM as a revenue forecast.

## 6. Technology and moat

For every claimed technical advantage, test:

`measured performance -> customer consequence -> economic consequence -> durability`

Compare named competitors and substitutes on common dimensions: specification, yield, reliability, total cost, qualification, installed capacity, utilization, customer access, capital needs, switching costs, intellectual property, and replication time. Distinguish a current advantage, an entry barrier, and a durable moat.

## 7. Financial quality

Reconcile revenue growth to gross profit and cash generation. Normalize grants, asset sales, fair-value changes, capitalization choices, stock compensation, restructuring, impairments, related-party transactions, minority interests, and other one-offs. Track receivable, inventory, payable and contract-liability days; maintenance and growth capex; leases and debt; covenants; dilution; and funding runway.

Use consolidated figures and segment or subsidiary figures consistently. Explain non-controlling interests and diluted shares before deriving per-share value.

## 8. Forecast and scenario discipline

Forecast from operational drivers. State sources or rationale for volume, price, mix, margin, capex, working capital, tax, net debt, and share count. Reconcile segment forecasts to consolidated revenue and earnings. Link bear, base, and bull cases to the same model architecture.

Scenarios must be coherent worlds. Each includes operating assumptions, timing, capital needs, valuation or return, signposts, and failure conditions. Do not change terminal multiples merely to force a desired outcome.

## 9. Research stop rule

Stop when additional work is unlikely to change the decision, major claims are triangulated, forecast drivers are bounded, valuation is cross-checked, contradictions are explained, and remaining unknowns are explicit. Do not mistake exhaustive fact collection for analytical completeness.
